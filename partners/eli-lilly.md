# Eli Lilly and Company

**Status: Prospective — no existing relationship with CitraChem.** This profile documents Eli Lilly as a partnership *candidate* surfaced by `research/briefs/2026-09-30-eli-lilly-trulicity-cliff.md`, not a confirmed partner. Do not cite this page as evidence CitraChem and Lilly have any agreement, meeting, or supply arrangement; the only confirmed CitraChem partner on record is Waystone Pharmaceuticals (LOI, April 2025, per `CLAUDE.md`).

## Summary
Eli Lilly and Company is a large-cap pharmaceutical company with diabetes and obesity (Mounjaro/tirzepatide, Zepbound) as its two largest revenue franchises — a combined $36.5B in 2025 sales, 56% of total company revenue [Fool.com/GuruFocus, cited in `research/briefs/2026-09-30-eli-lilly-trulicity-cliff.md`]. Trulicity (dulaglutide), an older GLP-1 agonist in the same franchise, faces US patent/exclusivity expiration in 2027 with ~$7.1B in annual revenue at risk per CitraChem's own Series A deck (`research/OPPORTUNITY-MAP.md`). Lilly's own confirmed answer to that gap is its next-generation GLP-1/GIP peptide-agonist pipeline (orforglipron, retatrutide) — not an externally sourced cannabinoid or terpenoid mechanism; no public evidence of any Lilly cannabinoid/ECS pipeline activity was found in this pass or the prior brief. Separately, and more relevant to a BD approach, Lilly runs a real, current, actively promoted external-innovation function — **Lilly Catalyze360** — that explicitly lists "Diabetes, Obesity and Cardiometabolic Diseases" as one of its named focus areas alongside Immunology, Neuroscience, and Oncology.

## (a) Lilly's external-innovation channel — verified
The brief's reference to a general `lilly.com/innovation` page was not itself found or verifiable (direct fetch of `lilly.com` is blocked by network egress rules in this session, and no page at that exact path surfaced in search). What **does** verify as real and current, via multiple independent, dated, third-party sources (search-snippet corroborated; direct `lilly.com` fetch blocked):

- **Lilly Catalyze360** is Lilly's current, actively-promoted external-innovation umbrella, built on three pillars: **Lilly Ventures** (investment arm), **Lilly Gateway Labs** (biotech incubator/co-working space; new Boston site opening 2026 alongside existing US locations), and **Lilly ExploR&D** (IND-enabling and early clinical development capability offered to external partners). Its named, explicitly listed focus areas include **Diabetes, Obesity and Cardiometabolic Diseases**, alongside Immunology, Neuroscience, Oncology, and several platform/technology categories [lilly.com/partners/lilly-catalyze360, search-corroborated 2026-09-30].
- **Named, current contact:** **Nisha Nanda, Ph.D.**, Group Vice President, Business Development, Head of Catalyze360 — joined Lilly in 2019 via the Loxo Oncology acquisition (previously Loxo's Chief Development Officer / R&D COO). In a FierceBiotech interview, Nanda states Catalyze360 is "pretty prolific" working with small companies and that all therapeutic areas are effectively on the table, not just Lilly's core four [FierceBiotech, "'We're pretty prolific': Lilly Catalyze360 looks to connect with emerging biotechs across 'all areas'" — publication date not independently confirmed (direct fetch blocked by egress rules); surfaced alongside a Feb 7, 2025-dated related story in search results, so plausibly early-to-mid 2025].
- **A live, funded signal of intent, not just a webpage:** Lilly and Andreessen Horowitz (a16z Bio + Health) launched a **$500M Biotech Ecosystem Venture Fund** (announced ~Jan 9–Feb 7, 2025), co-managed with a16z, with Catalyze360 resources (Ventures/Gateway Labs/ExploR&D) available to portfolio companies [BioSpace, BusinessWire, HLTH.com, dated Jan–Feb 2025].
- **A working submission channel:** `lilly.com/partners/collaborate-with-us` and `lilly.com/partners/connect` are live pages inviting external companies to describe their science/technology; Lilly Gateway Labs separately publishes a direct intake email, **gatewaylabs@lilly.com** [search-corroborated 2026-09-30; direct fetch of lilly.com blocked by egress rules in this session, so page content is snippet-corroborated, not independently read in full — verify directly before using in outward-facing material].
- **Legacy program, status unclear:** Lilly's older Open Innovation Drug Discovery (OIDD) platform (`openinnovation.lilly.com`, including the PD2/TD2 compound-screening initiative launched ~2008–2009) is well documented historically (800+ participants, 53,000+ compounds tested as of 2017 per one source) but no current (2024–2026) reference to it was found, and the domain did not resolve in this session (`getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND openinnovation.lilly.com`). Treat this as **likely superseded by Catalyze360/ExploR&D**, not a confirmed-dead program — don't reference it as an active channel without re-verification.

**Bottom line for (a):** there is a real, current, named external-innovation channel (Catalyze360, headed by Nisha Nanda) with diabetes/obesity/cardiometabolic explicitly in scope — this is a materially stronger finding than the brief's unverified `lilly.com/innovation` reference, and gives a legitimate, non-cold entry point.

## (b) Is the THCV/Hebrew University program far enough along to pitch?
**No — not yet, and this should be said plainly rather than oversold.** Per `research/OPPORTUNITY-MAP.md`'s research-programs table, CitraChem has an active internal derivative-research program starting from THCV with Hebrew University in "Obesity / Type 2 Diabetes," explicitly flagged as "not yet commercial MOUs" — i.e., a research direction, not a data package or compound ready to show a partner. Checking further:

- No public paper, patent, press release, or preprint tying **CitraChem + Hebrew University + THCV + obesity/diabetes** together was found in this pass. This appears to be non-public information sourced only from CitraChem's own internal Series A deck — appropriate for a private BD conversation to *mention*, but there is no external, citable evidence of results, a candidate molecule, or a timeline to point Lilly to.
- The existing outbound touch to Hebrew University (`outreach/2026-09-16-mccr-hebrew-university.md`) targets **Prof. Joseph Tam**, director of the Multidisciplinary Center for Cannabinoid Research (MCCR) and head of its Obesity and Metabolism Laboratory — but that touch is built around a *different*, independently published paper (Kočvarová et al. 2026, *BJP*, on CBD/CBG in steatotic liver disease/MASLD, not THCV). It's good evidence Hebrew University runs active, publication-grade obesity/metabolism cannabinoid research broadly, and plausible (not confirmed) that Prof. Tam's lab is the same group behind the THCV program in the Opportunity Map — but that link itself is unverified ("cross-check before treating the THCV programs... as a separate contact," per the Opportunity Map's own caveat), and no public THCV-specific output from that collaboration was found.
- Independently, the *scientific rationale* for THCV in obesity/T2D is well-established in the peer-reviewed literature on its own terms (e.g., Wargent et al., "The cannabinoid Δ9-tetrahydrocannabivarin (THCV) ameliorates insulin sensitivity in two mouse models of obesity," *Nutrition & Diabetes*, 2013, PMC3671751; a 2025 review in PMC12011981 on THCV in metabolic disorders) — useful as third-party scientific credibility for why a THCV derivative program is a reasonable bet, but this is general THCV literature, not CitraChem/Hebrew University-specific data.

**Recommendation:** don't lead a Lilly conversation with "we have a THCV/obesity derivative program with Hebrew University" as if it were a dossier — it's a direction, not yet a candidate molecule or dataset. The more honest and still-credible opening is CitraChem's **platform capability** (biomimetic semi-synthesis reaching rare cannabinoids like THCV at purity/scale extraction can't match, per `CLAUDE.md`) plus the Waystone precedent (a live case study of a pre-clinical ECS-focused company choosing CitraChem over agricultural extraction or de novo synthesis) as the credibility anchor, with the THCV/obesity-diabetes research interest mentioned as a *direction CitraChem is already resourcing*, not a result being offered for license or co-development today.

## Why a fit for CitraChem
- Lilly explicitly lists Diabetes, Obesity and Cardiometabolic Diseases as a Catalyze360 focus area, and its Trulicity patent cliff (2027, ~$7.1B at risk per `research/OPPORTUNITY-MAP.md`) sits inside that exact indication pair — a live, dated reason to reach out now rather than a generic cold approach.
- CitraChem already has an internal derivative-research direction (THCV, with Hebrew University) in the same indication pair — real (per CitraChem's own deck) even though not yet public or data-backed, giving a BD conversation a specific starting point rather than a blank "we sell pure cannabinoids" pitch.
- The realistic ask is narrow and appropriately scoped: an early-science-scouting conversation about CitraChem's custom-synthesis platform as an ingredient/derivative-supply option for ECS-adjacent metabolic-disease research, not a claim that CitraChem can replace or compete with Lilly's GLP-1/GIP peptide franchise.
- Genuinely honest caveat to carry into any actual outreach: Lilly's own chosen mechanism for this disease space is peptide agonism, not small-molecule ECS modulation, and no public Lilly cannabinoid pipeline activity exists — this is a speculative, exploratory BD angle, not a warm or expected-fit lead like Waystone was.
