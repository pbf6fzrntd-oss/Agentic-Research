# Battlecard: Lygos, Inc. (incl. "Lygos CBx" / Librede)

**Category:** vs. biosynthesis / fermentation cannabinoid platforms
**Last checked:** 2026-09-16

## Summary
Lygos, Inc. is a San Francisco Bay Area industrial synthetic-biology company (founded 2011, UC Berkeley bioengineering spinout) whose core, currently-promoted business is fermentation-derived specialty and industrial biochemicals — not cannabinoids. Lygos entered the cannabinoid space in January 2020 by acquiring Librede, Inc. and launched a "Lygos CBx" fermentation-based cannabinoid line (CBD, CBG, CBC, CBN, CBGA, CBCA, CBDA, CBDV) through 2021, with consumer-goods co-development deals (haircare, cosmetics, nutraceuticals). **No Lygos press release, product announcement, or partnership involving cannabinoids was found after 2021.** As of this check (September 2026), the `cbx.lygos.com` cannabinoid-brand subdomain does not resolve, and Lygos's current public-facing announcements (e.g., its September 2024 CJ Bio biorefinery partnership) describe only its industrial malonate/polyaspartate product lines (Ecoteria™, Soltellus™), with no mention of cannabinoids. **Inferred, not confirmed by a direct company statement:** the cannabinoid/Lygos CBx business appears dormant or discontinued in favor of Lygos's industrial biochemicals business; this could not be confirmed via a primary Lygos source, and it is possible cannabinoid work continues quietly without public announcement — treat this as a live-status question worth flagging to prospects rather than a settled fact.

## Pricing / Sourcing model
No public price list for cannabinoids was ever published; Lygos CBx operated on a B2B bulk-supply model, quoting per project, with historical fermentation batches reported at ~10,000 L scale yielding 100+ kg of cannabinoid product (source: Lygos, "Lygos Successfully Completes Commercial-Scale Fermentation Campaign," Business Wire, Oct 27, 2021). No cannabinoid pricing, MOQ, or lead-time information is currently discoverable from any primary source as of September 2026 — **status unverified, likely stale or defunct.** Lygos's current (2024-era) core business (Soltellus/Ecoteria industrial chemicals) is also quote-based B2B, sold through distributor Knowde and direct sales (source: Knowde product listings, accessed 2026; SpecialChem, accessed 2026).

## Capabilities
- **Historical (2020-2021) cannabinoid capability, per Lygos's own announcements:** proprietary yeast/microbial fermentation platform producing CBD, CBG, CBC, CBN, CBGA, CBCA, CBDA, and CBDV, described as "THC-free," consistent, and not sourced from agricultural cultivation (source: Business Wire, Oct 26, 2020 and Jan 19, 2021 releases). Named consumer-brand partnerships: MetaLabs (Korea, haircare, Dec 2020), High Beauty (cosmetics), Open Book Extracts (nutraceutical MOU) — all 2020-2021 announcements, no confirmed continuation found.
- **Current (2024-2026) core business, per Lygos's own materials:** fermentation-based production of malonic acid derivatives (Ecoteria™) and sodium polyaspartate biodegradable polymers (Soltellus™) for home care, personal care, agriculture, coatings, and fragrance-manufacturing applications — not pharmaceutical or cannabinoid-focused (source: Lygos/CJ BIO partnership announcement, Business Wire, Sept 10, 2024; Knowde and SpecialChem product pages, accessed 2026).
- September 2024: Lygos signed an MOU with CJ BIO for a commercial-scale biorefinery in Fort Dodge, Iowa, targeting up to 40,000 metric tons/year of Soltellus and Ecoteria (with stated potential to scale to 100,000 MT/yr), construction expected 18-24 months post-groundbreaking — this is Lygos's flagship, actively-promoted growth story as of the most recent primary-source announcement found (source: Business Wire, Sept 10, 2024).
- Third-party aggregators (Caplight, Tracxn — not primary sources) report ~$51.8M raised across 27 funding rounds; unverified against a Lygos primary source or SEC filing.
- Note for disambiguation: the well-documented fermented-cannabinoid supply relationship with **Cronos Group** is with **Ginkgo Bioworks**, not Lygos (source: Cronos Group Form 10-K, FY2023, SEC EDGAR) — do not conflate the two fermentation players when discussing this space.

## Where CitraChem wins
- **Active, current cannabinoid focus vs. an apparently dormant one.** CitraChem's platform is purpose-built and continuously operated for phytocannabinoids, terpenes, and custom terpenophenolic derivatives; Lygos's cannabinoid work has no confirmed public activity since 2021, while its company energy and capital (the 2024 CJ Bio Iowa biorefinery) is visibly directed at industrial malonates/polymers, not cannabinoid API supply.
- **Rare/novel cannabinoid and custom-derivative depth.** CitraChem's custom synthesis service is a live, standing offer (submit via citrachem.com/custom/) for a client's own candidate molecule; Lygos CBx's published menu (CBD, CBG, CBC, CBN, CBGA, CBCA, CBDA, CBDV) was last updated in a 2021 press release, with no evidence of newer or bespoke molecules being added since.
- **Pharma/regulated-buyer fit.** Lygos's confirmed cannabinoid partnerships (MetaLabs haircare, High Beauty cosmetics, Open Book Extracts nutraceuticals) were all consumer-product deals, not pharma/pre-clinical API relationships; CitraChem's ICP and reference deal shape (e.g., the Waystone Pharmaceuticals LOI) is explicitly pharma/biotech-facing.
- **No plant-derived agricultural precursor dependency ambiguity.** CitraChem's biomimetic route starts from plant-based essential oils in a controlled lab process (not cannabis cultivation); Lygos's fermentation route uses engineered microbes, a materially different process CitraChem should describe accurately, not conflate with its own — see honest gap below.

## Where they win / honest gaps
- **Fermentation/biosynthesis is a genuinely different, credible route CitraChem should not dismiss.** If Lygos's cannabinoid platform is in fact still operative (unconfirmed either way from public sources), microbial fermentation is capable of producing cannabinoids at scale without any plant-derived precursor at all — a distinct pitch from CitraChem's plant-based-essential-oil semi-synthesis, and some buyers may specifically want a precursor-free fermentation route for supply-chain or regulatory reasons.
- **Deep industrial-fermentation infrastructure and scale-up experience.** Lygos's demonstrated ability to execute a 40,000 MT/yr (scaling to 100,000 MT/yr) commercial biorefinery partnership (CJ BIO, Fort Dodge, Iowa, 2024) shows real large-volume fermentation scale-up capability, even if currently directed at non-cannabinoid chemistries — this is a credible signal of what the underlying platform could do if redirected.
- **Longer operating history than CitraChem in synthetic biology generally.** Founded 2011, with award-recognized biobased chemistry (DOE-recognized malonic acid innovation) — an established platform, even though its cannabinoid-specific track record is thin and unconfirmed as current.
- **Genuine uncertainty CitraChem should not overstate as "confirmed dead."** No public confirmation of discontinuation was found; sales/marketing teams should describe Lygos's cannabinoid status as "no public activity since 2021, current status unconfirmed" rather than asserting it has shut down, and should re-verify before a live competitive deal cites this.

- Lygos acquired Librede, Inc. in January 2020 and launched a fermentation-based cannabinoid product line branded "Lygos CBx" (CBD, CBG, CBC, CBN, CBGA, CBCA, CBDA, CBDV) in October 2020. Source: Business Wire, Jan 15, 2020 and Oct 26, 2020.
- No cannabinoid-related press release, product update, or partnership from Lygos has been found dated after 2021; the "cbx.lygos.com" subdomain does not currently resolve (checked Sept 16, 2026) — current status of Lygos's cannabinoid business is unconfirmed, not stated as discontinued by Lygos itself.
- Lygos's actively-promoted current business (as of its most recent primary-source announcement, September 2024) is industrial fermentation-derived malonates (Ecoteria™) and biodegradable polyaspartate polymers (Soltellus™), via an MOU with CJ BIO for a Fort Dodge, Iowa biorefinery targeting 40,000-100,000 metric tons/year — not cannabinoids. Source: Business Wire, Sept 10, 2024.
