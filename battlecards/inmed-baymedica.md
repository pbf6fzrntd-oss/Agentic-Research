# Battlecard: InMed Pharmaceuticals / BayMedica

**Category:** vs. biosynthesis/fermentation cannabinoid manufacturing (also touches CitraChem's own "rare cannabinoids" differentiator — read the overlap section carefully)
**Last checked:** 2026-09-16

## Summary

InMed Pharmaceuticals (Nasdaq: INM) is a Vancouver-based clinical-stage pharmaceutical company whose proprietary **IntegraSyn™** platform uses engineered *E. coli* to biosynthesize cannabigerolic acid (CBGA) — the "gateway" cannabinoid precursor — which is then enzymatically/chemically converted into various naturally-occurring cannabinoids. InMed acquired **BayMedica** (a synthetic-biology/yeast-fermentation rare-cannabinoid manufacturer) in October 2021 to combine biosynthesis, engineered-yeast fermentation, and chemical synthesis into one "three distinct and complementary" manufacturing toolkit sold commercially as rare-cannabinoid ingredients.

**Material status change (confirmed, 2026):** InMed's board approved winding down and exiting BayMedica's commercial cannabinoid-ingredient business on March 4, 2026, citing new federal hemp legislation (H.R. 5371, enacted Nov 12, 2025) that bans non-natural/lab-converted cannabinoids and puts BayMedica's rare, non-intoxicating cannabinoid inventory at legal risk starting Nov 12, 2026. BayMedica had substantially wound down all operating activities by June 30, 2026 (FY2026 fiscal year end) and terminated its commercial lease at the end of August 2026. Separately, InMed itself is being reverse-merged into **Mentari Therapeutics** (a migraine-drug biotech) in an all-stock deal signed May 19, 2026 and expected to close Q4 2026; pre-merger Mentari holders would own ~98.5% of the combined company, and InMed's legacy assets (including its cannabinoid drug candidates INM-901, INM-089, INM-755, and by extension the IntegraSyn/BayMedica IP) are carved out into a Contingent Value Rights (CVR) structure for potential future sale/license rather than continued as an operating cannabinoid-ingredient business.

**Net effect for a buyer evaluating this "competitor" today: BayMedica is not an active commercial rare-cannabinoid ingredient supplier as of this writing, and InMed's corporate identity/ticker is being absorbed into a non-cannabinoid, migraine-focused company.** Treat any BayMedica marketing material or product pages found via search as describing a business that has ceased ingredient sales, pending confirmation of a sale/license of the legacy assets.

- InMed/BayMedica never published a public price list; BayMedica sold rare/minor cannabinoid isolates (CBC, THCV, CBDV, CBG, CBL, CBT) wholesale/bulk on a quote basis via baymedica.com, directing buyers to "contact us" for pricing (confirmed via BayMedica product-page search snippets, checked 2026-09-16 — direct site fetch blocked in this session, so treat page content as last-indexed, not necessarily current).
- BayMedica FY revenue: $5.0M (fiscal year ended June 30, 2025) vs. $3.2M (fiscal year ended June 30, 2026, a partial/wind-down year), reported as discontinued operations in InMed's FY2026 financial results. Source: InMed "Reports Full Year Fiscal 2026 Financial Results and Provides Corporate Update," via Newsfile/BioSpace, dated ~September 2026.
- No current sourcing route: commercial operations wound down by June 30, 2026; lease terminated end of August 2026.

## Capabilities (as previously marketed — largely historical/discontinued)

- **IntegraSyn (InMed, in-house with Almac Group):** engineered *E. coli* strains express a proprietary enzyme system that assembles CBGA de novo from feedstock; InMed reported achieving 2 g/L cannabinoid yield (April 26, 2021, GlobeNewswire) and later 5 g/L at small scale "in advance of commercial-scale production" (June 17, 2021, GlobeNewswire) — both InMed's own reported figures, not independently verified. CBGA is described as convertible into multiple downstream naturally-occurring cannabinoids. Patent filings on the IntegraSyn enzyme/process were announced starting ~September 2020 (PR Newswire/Nasdaq).
- **BayMedica (acquired Oct 13, 2021):** used engineered brewer's-yeast fermentation (sugar feedstock, not *E. coli*) plus chemical synthesis and semi-synthesis to produce rare/minor cannabinoids (CBC, THCV, CBDV, CBG, CBL, CBT) at what BayMedica's own site described as "food-grade GMP-aligned quality systems," R&D-scale fermentation with claimed potential to scale to commercial volumes.
- Combined pitch (per InMed's 2021 acquisition press release): "complete manufacturing flexibility to select the most appropriate, cost-effective method based on the target cannabinoid" — i.e., a route-agnostic toolbox (biosynthesis, fermentation, chemical synthesis) rather than a single process.
- InMed's own pharmaceutical pipeline (INM-901 for Alzheimer's, INM-089 for dry AMD, INM-755 cannabinol cream for epidermolysis bullosa) used IntegraSyn-derived cannabinoids as drug candidates, distinct from BayMedica's commercial ingredient sales.

## Where CitraChem wins

- **Business continuity / supply reliability.** BayMedica's commercial ingredient business is confirmed wound down as of mid-2026, and InMed's cannabinoid assets are parked in a CVR pending a legacy-asset sale that may or may not happen. Any buyer citing BayMedica as a current source needs to be told this is inactive — CitraChem is an operating supplier, not one exiting the category under regulatory pressure.
- **Regulatory exposure profile.** BayMedica's wind-down was driven by H.R. 5371 reclassifying "non-natural" or lab-converted cannabinoids out of the legal hemp definition. CitraChem's biomimetic semi-synthesis starts from plant-based essential-oil precursors and targets botanically-identical, naturally-occurring cannabinoid structures — worth scientists/procurement independently confirming CitraChem's own products' status under the same statute, but the process rationale (following the plant's own biosynthetic logic rather than de novo microbial synthesis of "non-natural" analogs) is a materially different regulatory starting point than an engineered-*E.-coli*/lab-converted platform. (Note: this is CitraChem's inference about relative exposure, not a regulatory determination — do not represent it to a customer as legal advice.)
- **Feedstock/route:** CitraChem starts from plant-based essential oils and follows the plant's own biosynthetic logic (semi-synthesis); InMed/BayMedica start from bacterial (*E. coli*) or yeast fermentation of sugar feedstocks (de novo microbial biosynthesis) — a fundamentally different route than CitraChem's, even though both platforms target "botanically-identical"/"bioidentical" end molecules and both explicitly target rare/minor cannabinoids beyond CBD/CBG.
- **Custom synthesis for bespoke/novel molecules:** CitraChem's custom synthesis service (citrachem.com/custom/) takes a client's own candidate molecule/derivative; nothing in InMed/BayMedica's public materials describes an equivalent client-specified custom-derivative service — their platform was pitched as route flexibility across a fixed target list of naturally-occurring cannabinoids, not open-ended novel terpenophenolic derivative synthesis. (Absence of evidence, not confirmed absence — flagged as inferred from available materials, not a confirmed gap.)

## Where they win / honest gaps

- **Track record at claimed commercial-relevant fermentation titers (historical):** InMed publicly reported reaching 5 g/L cannabinoid yield via IntegraSyn in 2021 — a specific, dated, quantified process metric. CitraChem should be prepared to speak to its own yield/COA metrics with comparable specificity if a technical buyer asks for an apples-to-apples comparison; this battlecard does not have a public CitraChem yield figure to cite.
- **Multi-route flexibility (historical):** the combined InMed/BayMedica pitch — biosynthesis, yeast fermentation, and chemical synthesis under one roof, selecting whichever is cheapest for a given target molecule — is a broader manufacturing-method portfolio than CitraChem's single biomimetic semi-synthesis route, on paper. Whether that flexibility survives the wind-down/merger is unconfirmed.
- **GMP-aligned commercial ingredient sales history:** BayMedica had an actual revenue-generating wholesale/bulk ingredient business ($5.0M FY2025) selling into the health/wellness industry before winding down — a longer commercial sales track record in that specific channel than can be assumed for any newer entrant; CitraChem should not claim to have out-competed BayMedica commercially — BayMedica's exit was a regulatory/legislative decision by InMed's board, not a claim that CitraChem displaced them.
