# Battlecard: Willow Biosciences (now Atlas Energy Corp — cannabinoid business defunct/divested)

## Status flag (read first)

**Willow Biosciences is no longer an operating cannabinoid company and is not a live competitor.** The corporate entity that traded as Willow Biosciences (TSX/CSE: WLLW, OTCQB: CANSF) sold its entire cannabinoid biosynthesis business in April 2025 and renamed itself **Atlas Energy Corp** (TSXV: ATLE) in June 2025 — now an oil & gas royalty and streaming company with no cannabinoid operations. Its former biosynthesis IP, R&D team, and yeast-fermentation platform were sold to **Mycofeast Ltd**, a UK-based biomass-fermentation biotech whose public focus is animal-feed ingredients; there is no confirmed public evidence Mycofeast is commercializing cannabinoids post-acquisition (**inferred gap, not confirmed** — flagged below). Include this card in the "biosynthesis/fermentation" competitor category for historical/battlecard-completeness and to correct any stale claim (including from earlier research this session) that a Willow–Noramco cannabinoid supply relationship is current — it is not, and the two companies' exits from cannabinoids (Willow's 2022 JDA termination and divestiture; Noramco's 2026 acquisition by Siegfried) were separate, unrelated events years apart.

## Summary

Willow Biosciences was a Canadian (Calgary-based) biotech that built a proprietary yeast-fermentation ("biosynthesis") platform to produce cannabinoids — CBD, CBG, THC, and rare "varin" cannabinoids (CBGV, CBDV, THCV) — without cannabis cultivation. It began with a 2019 joint development agreement with Noramco (Willow: strain engineering/biosynthesis; Noramco: scale-up, regulatory, marketing, and distribution), which Willow terminated in December 2022 to retain full IP control and pursue rare cannabinoids independently. Facing an inability to raise financing on favorable terms, Willow launched a strategic review in January 2025, sold its entire cannabinoid operating subsidiary (Epimeron USA, including the biosynthesis IP and R&D team) to Mycofeast Ltd for US$2.76M in April 2025, and then completed a $30M recapitalization and rebrand to Atlas Energy Corp — an oil & gas royalty/streaming company — in June 2025. As of this check (September 2026), "Willow Biosciences" the cannabinoid company does not exist.

Not applicable — company is defunct as a cannabinoid supplier. No current pricing exists because there is no current commercial offering. *(Source: Willow Biosciences press release archive via newswire.ca/prnewswire.com, 2019–2023, as indexed by web search September 2026; historical claim, not independently re-verified against a live document due to publisher-side access restrictions during this research pass.)*

## Capabilities (historical, as an active company through ~2024)

- Proprietary yeast-based fermentation ("biosynthesis") platform engineered to produce cannabinoids directly in engineered yeast strains rather than via plant cultivation/extraction or de novo chemical synthesis.
- Achieved >99% purity CBG via a proprietary downstream purification process, at commercial fermentation scale (>10,000 L), per a 2021 company press release.
- Expanded platform beyond major cannabinoids (CBD, CBG, THC) to rare/minor "varin" cannabinoids — CBGV, CBDV, THCV — which occur at <1% of plant biomass and are difficult/costly to source via cultivation.
- 2019–2022: joint development agreement with Noramco for CBD specifically (Willow = biosynthesis/strain development; Noramco = scale-up, regulatory submission, marketing, and distribution). Terminated by Willow in December 2022; Willow retained full ownership of platform IP going forward.
- No independent, current (2025–2026) source confirms this platform or IP is being actively commercialized by its new owner, Mycofeast — **this is a gap in available information, not a confirmed "still active" claim.**

## Where CitraChem wins

- **Still operating and shipping.** CitraChem is an active, going-concern manufacturer; Willow Biosciences' cannabinoid business no longer exists as a commercial supply option at all — there is no live account to open, no catalog to order from, and no roadmap to evaluate. Any buyer currently sourcing from or evaluating Willow as a fermentation-biosynthesis alternative needs a replacement supplier now.
- **Continuity and platform breadth.** CitraChem's biomimetic semi-synthesis process (from plant-based essential-oil precursors) extends to rare cannabinoids and bespoke terpenophenolic derivatives with an in-house custom synthesis service — the kind of rare-cannabinoid demand (varins, novel derivatives) Willow was chasing in its final years before its 2025 wind-down, without the platform-continuity risk a buyer would now face with Willow's successor entity.
- **No dependency on a single JDA partner's fate.** Willow's original commercialization plan depended on Noramco for scale-up/regulatory/distribution; when that partnership ended (2022) and financing dried up (2025), the cannabinoid business did not survive independently. CitraChem is not structured as a platform-tech-plus-distribution-partner arrangement of that kind.

## Where they win / honest gaps (historical, while active)

- **Fermentation/biosynthesis is a genuinely different route than CitraChem's semi-synthesis**, and Willow's historical >99%-purity CBG and high-titer (>10,000 L) fermentation data points (2021, self-reported, not independently audited in sources reviewed here) suggest the underlying yeast-biosynthesis approach can hit high purity and scale for at least some cannabinoids — a data point worth knowing even though the company that generated it is gone.
- **Honest gap:** CitraChem has not publicly disclosed a side-by-side purity/cost comparison against fermentation-route cannabinoids; any claim of superiority over biosynthesis broadly (as opposed to specifically vs. the now-defunct Willow) should cite a current, active biosynthesis competitor and a dated source, not this one.

- Willow's cannabinoid yeast-fermentation biosynthesis business, IP, and R&D team (operating subsidiary Epimeron USA) were sold to Mycofeast Ltd, a UK biomass-fermentation biotech, for US$2.76 million, completed April 30, 2025.
- Willow's 2019 joint development agreement with Noramco for yeast-based CBD biosynthesis (Willow: strain/biosynthesis development; Noramco: scale-up, regulatory, marketing, distribution) was terminated by Willow in December 2022, three years before Willow's 2025 divestiture and unrelated to Noramco's later 2026 acquisition by Siegfried.
- Historical (2021, self-reported, not independently audited) platform claim: Willow produced CBG at greater than 99% purity at greater than 10,000-liter fermentation scale, yielding tens of kilograms.
- No confirmed public source as of September 2026 shows Mycofeast actively commercializing cannabinoids post-acquisition; Mycofeast's public focus is fermentation-derived animal-feed ingredients — this is an inferred gap, not a confirmed discontinuation.
