# Bristol Myers Squibb — FAAH/MAGL Pipeline (Irafamdastat / BMS-986368)

## 1. Target & angle

Bristol Myers Squibb is a named "Intel Inside" big-pharma target in `research/OPPORTUNITY-MAP.md`, but is disqualified twice already (Eliquis — anticoagulant, no ECS mechanism; Opdivo — checkpoint inhibitor, no ECS mechanism). A garbled SEC-filing search snippet from the broader 2026-09-30 patent-cliff sweep referenced FAAH and MAGL in a BMS pipeline table with an illegible indication tag. This brief runs that thread down: BMS does have a real, active, dual FAAH/MAGL inhibitor program — **irafamdastat (BMS-986368, formerly CC-97489)** — currently in Phase 2 trials for two indications, one of which (Alzheimer's disease) sits directly on CitraChem's own disease-target matrix.

## 2. Verified findings

**The compound is real and its provenance is now fully traceable** (via `WebSearch`; direct fetches to sec.gov, bms.com, and pharmaceutical-technology.com were egress-blocked, consistent with every other pass in this project — findings below rely on search-result snippets from multiple independent secondary sources that corroborate each other, plus ClinicalTrials.gov listings):

- **Origin:** Discovered by **Abide Therapeutics** (San Diego serine-hydrolase-platform biotech) as **ABX-1772**, a dual MGLL(MAGL)/FAAH inhibitor, originally positioned by Abide for **epilepsy**. Abide and Celgene had a 2014 strategic collaboration; in **March 2018**, Celgene took an **exclusive worldwide license** to ABX-1772 specifically (a separate, smaller deal than Celgene's option to acquire Abide outright, which was dropped at the same time — Abide was instead acquired by Lundbeck in 2019 for a different compound, the MAGL-selective ABX-1431/lorundrostat-unrelated program). Sources: BioCentury ("Celgene licenses preclinical candidate from Abide"), FierceBiotech ("Celgene drops option to buy Abide, bags preclinical drug"), Pharma Focus Asia, American Pharmaceutical Review — all describing the same March 2018 license.
- **Into BMS:** Celgene was acquired by Bristol Myers Squibb in November 2019. The compound carried Celgene's internal numbering (**CC-97489**) and now carries BMS's (**BMS-986368**), consistent with BMS's standard post-acquisition renumbering pattern. Current ClinicalTrials.gov listings show the sponsor as **"Celgene Corporation, a Bristol-Myers Squibb Company"** — i.e., this is an in-license folded into BMS via the Celgene acquisition, not a BMS-originated discovery program and not a recent new deal.
- **Mechanism:** Dual inhibitor of FAAH and MAGL, IC50 ≤100 nM (FAAH) and 100 nM–1 μM (MAGL) — both are the endocannabinoid-degrading enzymes named in the garbled snippet; inhibiting them raises endogenous anandamide/2-AG levels rather than supplying exogenous cannabinoids. INN name: **irafamdastat**.
- **Current clinical activity (confirmed active, not historical):**
  - **Phase 2, Multiple Sclerosis Spasticity** — NCT06782490, "BALANCE-MSS-1," 4-arm, 3 doses vs. placebo, ~200 participants, recruiting across US/EU/Canada/Australia/Puerto Rico, enrollment started March 2025, primary completion estimated May 2027.
  - **Phase 2, Agitation in Alzheimer's Disease** — NCT06808984, "BALANCE-AAD-1," 120 participants, 48–60 sites, started June 2025, estimated completion January 2028, sponsor listed as Celgene Corporation (a BMS company).
  - Multiple ongoing **Phase 1 PK/safety studies** (healthy volunteers, elderly, Japanese and Chinese ethnicity cohorts, drug-interaction studies) — NCT06170723, NCT06411730, NCT07700329 — consistent with an active, currently-progressing program, not a shelved one.
  - Originally studied for epilepsy (Abide's original hypothesis); no clearly confirmed *current* active epilepsy trial was found in this pass — the program's live, confirmed indications today are MS spasticity and Alzheimer's-associated agitation.
- **Not independently page-verified:** none of this was confirmed against a primary BMS or SEC source directly (egress-blocked); it rests on convergent secondary reporting (BioCentury, FierceBiotech, ClinicalTrials.gov listings, Multiple Sclerosis News Today, patsnap/delveinsight pipeline trackers) that agree on the same facts from different angles, which is a reasonable confidence bar per this project's standing constraint but should be flagged as such.

## 3. Fit assessment — weak/indirect, despite a real and current program

This is a genuine, current, ECS-mechanism BMS program, and two of its indications map directly onto CitraChem's disease-target matrix (**Alzheimer's Disease** — listed explicitly; **Neurodegenerative disease** — listed explicitly; MS spasticity is adjacent but not itself on the matrix). That said, the fit to CitraChem's actual product line is weak, for a specific reason worth stating plainly:

**Irafamdastat is not a phytocannabinoid, terpene, or terpenophenolic derivative — it's a synthetic serine-hydrolase-inhibitor small molecule (urea/carbamate chemotype) from Abide's enzyme-inhibitor discovery platform.** CitraChem's platform (per `CLAUDE.md`) is biomimetic semi-synthesis of cannabinoids and terpenes from plant-based essential-oil precursors, plus custom synthesis of "novel/rare cannabinoids and terpenophenolic derivatives" — a different chemotype and a different mechanism (supplying exogenous cannabinoid/terpene structures vs. inhibiting endocannabinoid-degrading enzymes). There is no indication CitraChem's manufacturing platform produces or could readily pivot to producing FAAH/MAGL-inhibitor chemical matter, and no evidence found that BMS's irafamdastat program sources — or needs — any exogenous phytocannabinoid ingredient (as a comparator, combination-study reagent, or otherwise). That specific "does BMS need a cannabinoid reference standard for this program" question was not confirmable via search; flagging as unconfirmed rather than asserting it either way.

So: real program, real indication-matrix overlap, but **no found ingredient-supply or custom-synthesis opening** — this is a mechanism-adjacency signal (BMS is demonstrably not "ECS-blind," which matters for a future BD conversation's framing) rather than a product-fit lead. It does not, on its own, change BMS's disqualified status for a cold "Intel Inside" ingredient pitch. It is also not a competitive-intel concern — BMS producing a synthetic enzyme inhibitor doesn't make it a producer of CitraChem's phytocannabinoid/terpene product class, unlike the Alterola Biotech/DeFloria precedent.
