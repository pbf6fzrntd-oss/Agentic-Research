## 2. Verified findings

**The two papers — confirmed, both from PubMed/search-snippet corroboration (direct PMC/DOI page fetch was `EGRESS_BLOCKED`, so titles/DOIs are search-snippet-sourced, not independently page-verified):**

1. "Combined effects of cannabidiol and Δ9-tetrahydrocannabinol alleviate migraine-like symptoms in mice" — *Cephalalgia*, Vol. 45(2), February 2025. DOI: 10.1177/03331024251314487. PMID 39988876, PMCID PMC12269478. Selected as the journal's February Editor's Choice. Tested CBD (100 mg/kg) and THC (1 mg/kg) alone and combined in CD1 mice (light aversion/photophobia, squint, periorbital tactile hypersensitivity triggered by CGRP and sodium nitroprusside), plus optogenetically-induced cortical spreading depolarization (CSD) in FHM1 mutant mice on a C57BL/6 background (the FHM1/CSD arm is the direct methodological link to the Leiden group, whose mouse models these are). Core finding: 100:1 CBD:THC combination outperformed either compound alone on several endpoints, no adverse cognitive/emotional/motor effects.
2. "Cannabinoids rescue migraine symptoms caused by central CGRP administration in mice" — *Cephalalgia*, Vol. 45(11), November 2025. DOI: 10.1177/03331024251392103. PMID 41182862. Same 100:1 CBD:THC combination (100 mg/kg CBD / 1 mg/kg THC), tested against centrally-administered CGRP in CD1 mice; rescued light aversion in both sexes, rescued CGRP/SNP-induced squint in males.
